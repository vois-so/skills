# Script patterns for a faceless Short

## Word budget

| Words | Length at the default pace | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 110 | about 31 to 35 s | One fact and one example |
| 135 | about 38 to 42 s | Three short points |
| 160 | about 45 to 50 s | A story in three steps |

The pace of this skill is about 3.2 to 3.6 words a second, because the script removes the silence at the ends of each take. Use `"speed": 0.95` for a calm Short. Use `"speed": 1.1` for an energetic Short. Then examine the length again.

## Hook types

| Type | Structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surprise | A statement that seems incorrect | "Your phone battery is lying to you." |
| Mistake | "You are doing X wrong." | "You are washing your rice wrong." |
| Question | One question with a short answer | "Why is the sky dark at night?" |
| Number | A number and a promise of a list | "Three words that end an argument." |
| Story | The middle of a story | "I tried to learn piano in thirty days." |
| Contrast | Two things that do not agree | "Gold is soft. So why is it money?" |

Rules for each hook:

- 5 to 8 words. It must stop by 2.0 seconds.
- The first word is a strong word. Not "Hi", "So", "Today" or the channel name.
- The next lines must prove it. A hook that the video does not prove is clickbait. The viewer leaves, and the platform sees that.
- Do not promise views, followers, money or a result for the viewer.

## Script shapes

### Three points (advice, facts)

```text
hook     Three words that end an argument.
setup    Most fights go on because nobody stops first.
point 1  The first word is "maybe". It gives the other person room.
point 2  The second is "and". It replaces "but", and it keeps both views on the table.
point 3  The third is "thanks". Use it when the other person makes a good point.
close    Maybe, and, thanks. Try one of them in your next argument.
```

### Myth and truth

```text
hook     Your phone battery is lying to you.
setup    That percentage is a guess, built from voltage and temperature.
truth    Your phone measures the voltage and compares it with a table.
example  In the cold, the voltage drops, so the guess drops too.
close    So charge it when it suits you, not when the number says so.
```

### Story in three steps (the story of the user)

```text
hook     I tried to learn piano in thirty days.
step 1   Day one, I could not play a single scale without a mistake.
step 2   Day ten, I stopped practicing songs and practiced only the hard bars.
step 3   Day thirty, I played the full piece for my family, slowly.
close    The trick was not more practice. It was practice on the hard parts.
```

Tell a story only if it is the story of the user. Or, the user must give the facts or a source. Do not make up names, dates or numbers.

## Rules for the ear

- One sentence for each line. Most sentences below 15 words.
- Write numbers, symbols and abbreviations as the voice must say them.
- Use "you". Speak to one person.
- Use commas for short pauses. The caption cues also break at commas.
- Do not use brackets, emoji or slashes. The voice can say them.
- A name that the voice says incorrectly goes in `say`, respelled. Keep the correct name in `text` for the captions.

## Close and last instruction

- Close with the one point again, in different words.
- For a loop, make the last line lead back to the hook. The viewer then hears the hook again, and it means more.
- A last instruction to the viewer is optional. Use one sentence: "Follow for part two." or "Save this for later."
- Do not tell the viewer that the video will get views, money or followers for them.

## Before you render

1. Count the words: 110 to 160. The script also counts them.
2. Read the hook aloud with a timer. It must be 2 seconds or less.
3. Examine each fact against the source that the user gave.
4. Remove all promises of views, followers or money.
