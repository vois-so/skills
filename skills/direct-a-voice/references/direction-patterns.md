# Direction patterns

Use these directions with `--instruct` on Omni or Craft. Each one has less than 2,000 characters and no brackets or parentheses. Start with a pattern. Then change one or two words for the line.

## Before you write a direction

1. Write the purpose of the paragraph above it, in plain words. Examples: "make the listener trust us", "let the surprise land", "slow down for the safety step". Then write the direction for that purpose.
2. Give the voice a sequence, not a summary. "Calm, then a little faster on the last sentence" is better than "dynamic".
3. Use less performance for text that the listener must trust. Policy, legal, safety and technical text is less credible when each sentence has much energy.
4. Make takes of three parts of the script, not of one test sentence. Use a usual change of subject, an explanation that is not easy and the most important moment.
5. Listen to the takes in sequence. A take that is good alone can be too fast after the take before it.

## Narration

| Content | Direction |
|---|---|
| Audiobook, fiction | "warm storyteller, relaxed pace, clear and close, as if to one listener" |
| Audiobook, non-fiction | "clear and confident, steady pace, friendly, no drama" |
| Documentary | "calm and serious, slow, low energy, room for each fact" |
| Documentary, the most important fact | "quiet and slow, then hold back a moment before the last words, restrained awe" |
| History | "measured and thoughtful, a storyteller who knows the end" |
| Children's story | "bright and gentle, playful, a smile in the voice, slower than usual" |
| Bedtime or sleep | "very soft and slow, low energy, gentle, long pauses between sentences" |
| Meditation | "slow, calm, soft and even, unhurried breaths between phrases" |

## Explainers and tutorials

| Content | Direction |
|---|---|
| Software tutorial | "friendly and clear, steady pace, patient, like a helpful colleague" |
| Explainer for an app | "confident and warm, medium pace, a little upbeat" |
| Course lesson | "clear teacher voice, calm, encouraging, slow on new terms" |
| Safety step | "slow and firm, serious, clear on each word, no smile" |
| Error message | "calm and direct, steady, reassuring at the end" |
| Recap | "relaxed and warm, a little faster, light" |

## Marketing

| Content | Direction |
|---|---|
| Ad, upbeat | "bright and energetic, quick pace, a smile in the voice" |
| Ad, premium | "low and smooth, slow, confident, understated" |
| The last line of an ad | "direct and warm, clear, a little more energy on the last words" |
| Testimonial read | "natural and sincere, conversational, not salesy" |
| Launch announcement | "excited but controlled, building energy to the last sentence" |

## Podcasts and conversation

| Content | Direction |
|---|---|
| Host open | "warm and upbeat, conversational, a smile in the voice" |
| Interview question | "curious and relaxed, a little slower, open" |
| Aside or joke | "amused, light, quick, almost laughing" |
| A sad topic | "calm and grounded, slower, low energy, sincere" |
| Sign-off | "warm and relaxed, slower, grateful" |

## News and briefings

| Content | Direction |
|---|---|
| News read | "neutral and clear, steady brisk pace, authoritative" |
| Urgent news | "urgent but controlled, faster, firm" |
| Company update | "calm and confident, clear, plain" |
| Bad news | "calm, slow, serious and kind, no smile" |

## Characters and emotion

| Emotion | Direction |
|---|---|
| Joy | "bright, quick, rising energy, a laugh close to the surface" |
| Quiet pride | "calm and warm, unhurried, a small smile" |
| Sadness | "soft and slow, low energy, voice a little heavy" |
| Grief | "holding back tears, slow, voice trembling slightly" |
| Anger, held back | "low and tight, slow, controlled, each word firm" |
| Open anger | "loud and sharp, fast, hard consonants" |
| Afraid | "quiet and fast, breathy, tense" |
| Surprise | "sudden lift in energy, quick, wide-eyed" |
| Sarcasm | "dry and flat, slow, a little amused" |
| Tired | "low energy, slow, a sigh in the voice" |
| Suspicion | "slow and quiet, careful, a little cold" |
| Relief | "a long breath out, then lighter and warmer" |
| Disbelief to joy | "quiet disbelief, then joy on the last sentence" |

## Change in a line

Use a change only if the line is short. For a long line, divide it and give each part its own direction.

| Change | Direction |
|---|---|
| Stronger at the end | "start calm, build energy to the last sentence" |
| Softer at the end | "start firm, then soften on the last sentence" |
| Slow at the end | "steady pace, slow down on the last words" |
| Turn | "light at first, then serious on the last sentence" |

## Strength

| Strength | Words to add |
|---|---|
| Small | "a little", "slightly", "a hint of" |
| Moderate | Use no strength word. |
| Large | "very", "strongly", "fully" |

Start with no strength word. Add one only if the take is too flat or too strong.

## Pace for each type of content

Use these ranges as a start. Measure the take: words divided by minutes.

| Content | Words a minute |
|---|---|
| Audiobooks and calm narration | 130 to 150 |
| Educational podcasts | 140 to 160 |
| Conversational podcasts | 150 to 170 |
| Video explainers | 160 to 180 |

Keep one usual pace. Make a definition or an emotional turn slower. Then go back to the usual pace. Use `--speed` (0.5 to 2.0) for a measured change. A direction about pace is not a measurement.

## What a direction cannot do

- It cannot put a sound at a specified word, for example a cough or a laugh.
- It cannot change the speaker into a different person. Use Voice Design (Pro) for a new voice.
- It cannot set an accent reliably.
- It cannot correct a word. Use the pronunciation dictionary or a different spelling.
- It cannot change the text. The voice says only the words in the text.
