# Rules for the ear

A viewer hears narration one time, at the speed of the voice. They cannot read the sentence again. These rules make a line easy to hear and easy for the voice to say.

## Sentences

- Put one idea in each sentence.
- Keep most sentences below 20 words. Use a short sentence after a long sentence.
- Put the subject and the verb near the start of the sentence.
- Use the active voice: "Vois Studio renders the line", not "the line is rendered".
- Put the new or important word at the end of the sentence. The voice puts stress there.

## Words

- Use the words that a person says in a conversation. "Use", not "utilize". "Start", not "commence".
- Write numbers as the voice must say them, when the number is not simple. "Two thousand twenty-six" or "twenty twenty-six" are different. Select one.
- Write symbols as words: "percent", "and", "dollars".
- Write an abbreviation as letters with spaces ("A P I") or as a word ("Nasa"), as a person says it.
- Put a name that the voice says incorrectly in `say`, respelled. Keep the correct name in `text` for the captions.

## Pauses and pace

- A full stop gives a longer pause than a comma. Use them to set the rhythm.
- Do not use brackets, slashes or emoji. The voice can say them or skip them.
- Plan about 2.5 words for each second (150 words a minute). A calm read is slower. An energetic read is faster.
- For a scene with a long visual, set `minSeconds`. Do not add words.

## Tone

- Expressive makes the sound of a small set of tags in the text: `[laugh]`, `[chuckle]`, `[gasp]`, `[sigh]`, `[groan]`, `[cough]`, `[clear throat]`, `[sniff]`, `[shush]`. Use them only when the sound helps.
- Omni and Craft use a direction in plain words: put it in `instruct`. Keep it short and concrete: "warm and unhurried, a small smile". Do not use brackets or parentheses in it.
- Fast, Multilingual and Sonic do not use tags or directions. The words and the punctuation give the tone.
